01One law, two ledgers

Everything below is downstream of one rule, and it is the only rule in the game that can be proved rather than argued about.

Every ton in the game was put in the ground at genesis. From then on it can only move — out of the crust, into a warehouse, into a hold, across a lane, through a factory that changes what it is, and finally into a mouth or a slag heap. It is never minted.

A second ledger says the same of money: credits are minted at genesis and never again. Both are checked by an auditor that rolls every pool in the universe into one column — crusts, warehouses, holds, hull structure, wreck fields, the sink, and the two pools the player stands in — and compares the total. A pool left out of that call reads exactly like a leak, which is the correct failure: forgetting to count a warehouse is losing track of the mass in it.

The check is on the grand total, not per material, because transformation is the whole point of industry. A smelter is supposed to destroy ferrite and create steel. What no process may do is change how many tons there are.

02The menus

The menu tree is konex's, kept deliberately: single player with three modes, options for display and player, and the save/load pair. Trader is the mode this project added — the commodity game with the reentry landing under it.

03Flight, war and the map

The flight view is konex's top-down arena, unchanged in feel: arrows turn and thrust, space fires, X brakes, Shift targets the nearest enemy. What changed is what the other ships are doing there.

04The voyage: warp, deorbit, reentry, takeoff

Getting to a planet's surface is four screens and about a minute of flying. Each is a real phase of the same trajectory rather than a transition.

The reentry corridor

This is the piece the project is really about, and the screen with the most physics in it. A 350-tonne freighter has the ballistic coefficient of a falling anvil; what makes it flyable is an MHD plasma aeroshell — a magnetic bubble inflated ahead of the nose that stands the shock off and sheds the heat.

05The concourse and its six screens

Landing ends on the concourse. Six sub-screens hang off it, and Esc from any of them returns here rather than opening the game menu.

06The Governor's Desk — four tabs

G at any dock. If you bought the first building at a world you hold its charter and the desk is writable; otherwise it is read-only and you are watching somebody else govern.

07The zero-sum economy

Under every screen above there is a simulation of everywhere: 109 ports digging, refining, eating, growing and trading, with roughly 195 hulls carrying the difference between them. It runs whether you are looking at it or not, and landing burns days — so a long delivery comes back to a market that has moved.

Matter: 30 materials in six tiers

The ordering is load-bearing in exactly one place: the first eight entries are the commodity board, in order, so a planet's warehouse and a ship's hold are a prefix of a full material vector and convert by copying.

Two predicates carry the travel rules, for two quite different reasons. Hot — pure lithium and heavylith travel in a shielded cask or not at all, because a cask that survives a jump costs more than the ton inside it, which is what pins the finishing plant beside the breeder rather than beside the customer. Bulkable — steel, concentrate, acid and hydraulic fluid are refined but ship like goods, because a city eats steel and the hot worlds import chemicals or run nothing.

Mass flow, every branch

Mass only ever flows left to right, except at the two return loops. The crust is the only source and it is finite; slag is the only grave. Everything in between is a change of identity at constant tonnage.

Industry by composition

Seventeen chains are built from twenty-three primitives by one operator:

func (m *Module) Then(next *Module) *Module

Plug m's outputs into next's inputs; get back a module whose ports are whatever the pair could not satisfy internally. Because the result is itself a module, it plugs into a third the same way — a supermodule is not a special kind of thing with its own rules, it is a module that remembers what it was built from.

Two properties, both tested: mass balances, and the chain runs at its bottleneck, recorded at composition time because that is the only moment the shortfall is visible. And nobody authored a speciality — a world ranks the chains its own rocks could back and stands up the best two. A world that can smelt and fabricate has a chip industry; nobody wrote "chip industry" down.

The lithium cycle

Fuel is radioactive heavy lithium, and it comes off a five-stage line in two forms that are not interchangeable. Which one a hull can burn is a question about the reactor bolted into it — so the reactor decides which refineries in the galaxy are your suppliers.

One tonne of spodumene is about 0.13 t of finished fuel across the whole line. The waste is slag and it is counted.

Hostile worlds

A world's radiological dose and its spodumene seam are drawn from one number — the single place in the game where two facts about a world are forced to be the same fact. A clean world has no spodumene at all, however rich it is otherwise; a hot world's reserve scales with exactly how hot it is.

Why a refinery is founded with money

A licensed world opens with working capital sized to its own line. Without it the hot worlds sit in a trap with no exit from inside: no chemicals → no fuel → no revenue → no chemicals. A port buys only what its treasury covers, so a broke world does not send a distress signal — it quietly declines the cargo and the courier flies on. One refinery ran a 63 t/day line at 9 % of capacity for a simulated year and nothing anywhere said why.

Prices as scarcity

Price is cover: how many days of real demand the warehouse holds. A short world pays up to 3×, a glutted one as little as 0.35×, and a producer sells cheap — which is what a producer is, and what gives a route its direction.

Two steepenings, each with an argument. Rations, ×7 — people will pay anything for the next meal, which turns a famine into the best-paying route on the board and gets it relieved without anybody scripting relief. A sited world's feedstock, ×5 — a refinery short of acid is not inconvenienced, it is shut.

And every finished good has an appetite, which is not decoration. The first cut gave chips, ore and steel no consumer, so they piled up in warehouses nobody would ever need them from and the whole network went quiet on day 104 with every hull idle. A commodity with no sink stops being traded the moment the first warehouse fills.

The trade loop

The order is the causal order and it matters: you cannot refine ore you have not dug, sell goods you have not made, or route a hull to a shortage this morning's production just filled.

Five kinds of agent

And the merchant fleet sizes itself. It opens proportional to worlds held — a trade network's size is a property of the map, not a constant somebody typed — and then every day each colour measures berth pressure, the unserved margin still on its board per hull afloat. Above 90 k cr/hull a yard presses plate; below 22 k it breaks a ship up and puts the tonnage back on the shelf. Nothing is minted: growth costs somebody a warehouse.

08Reentry physics

The magnetopause

The envelope the ship flies behind is not a heat shield, it is a magnetic bubble. The coil's dipole pushes outward with magnetic pressure; the oncoming flow pushes inward with ram pressure; and the stand-off is where they balance. Because a dipole falls off as r⁻³, its pressure falls as r⁻⁶:

    r_mp / R_n  =  ( p_mag / p_flow ) ^ 1/6        p_mag = B²/2μ₀

A bare dipole is not a closed bottle. It has two axial cusps where the field lines run into the vehicle and flow funnels straight down them onto the nose — which is what the multipole ring is for.

The confinement ladder

Nobody buys their way out of that exponent. To stand a shock off twice as far you need sixty-four times the magnetic pressure. What a yard can sell is a fix for one of four distinct ways the balance goes wrong — and each is a different piece of hardware because each is a different piece of physics.

Failure modeOutfitThe hook
the field is too weakHTS coil rewind +0.45 Tstand-off ∝ B^⅓ — honest, expensive, diminishing
the bottle leaks at the polesMultipole cusp ringplugs the cusps: more usable pressure, and two thirds less wake drag, which is where it really earns its price
the pillow is symmetricPhased steering arraydrives current asymmetrically and leans the envelope: +45 % commandable L/D, and MHD grip several km higher. Paid for on the power ledger
the air is too thin to gripSeed injection ringMHD needs a conducting continuum; high up there is nothing to push against however hard you push. Put the mass there yourself — inertial confinement, the only rung that works where the magnetic ones do not

That fourth rung is tested by the property that justifies it: at 88 km the injection ring beats tripling the coil, because tripling a field that has nothing to grip buys nothing at all.

09Ship reactors

A tonne of heavy lithium carries the same energy wherever it is loaded. What decides how much of it a ship can use is the machine it is loaded into.

ClassTakesBurnupRangeBreedsMass
Thermal pile — light-water moderatedPellets5.5 %×1.0—stock
Sodium fast loop — unmoderated, molten saltMelt19.5 %×3.5—+7 t
Shipboard breeder — fast loop + fertile blanketMelt25.0 %×4.50.35 t/t+16 t

The breeder is the third uphill loop in the game: it burns its charge and transmutes fertile lithium from a blanket it is carrying into fresh fissile material. It does not mint anything — the tonnage comes out of the blanket — which is precisely the distinction the books care about.

Not yet wired partial

Pellets and Melt are traded, priced and eaten by populations, but a hull's own jump does not burn them yet. Until it does, the range multiplier above is a shop label rather than a constraint you feel. It is the highest-value small piece of work outstanding.

One causal chain explains all six broken rows:

one world per system  →  copper is a one-jump good with nowhere to go
                      →  chips at 7% of demand
                      →  shipyards at 0% (chips are 15% of the yard recipe)
                      →  no hull plate
                      →  the fleet cannot grow however hard the board pays
                      →  too few hulls to fly acid to 15 scattered refineries
                      →  fuel at 2% of demand

Everything downstream of the first line is a symptom. Three bodies per system — one planet, one station, one nebula or asteroid field — is not a content item; it is the fix for the economy's only structural fault. Nothing above is a correctness bug. It is a map that cannot support the economy standing on it.

11Developer screens

The drop-down console (`) carries the economy: economy, routes, journal, world <id>, trifecta, standings, day <n>, govern, doctrine, priority, policy, tune, seed, plus konex's originals. GONEX_CMD="day 200;economy" runs them at boot and GONEX_SEED reproduces a universe exactly.

Animations none exist

Stand-in loops would do for all of these; the point is that several important events currently happen with no visual at all.

  • Hyperjump — entering and leaving the tunnel (the tunnel itself exists)
  • Harvester working a seam — the only mining the player can watch
  • Scoop — a hull lifting a wreck field, currently silent
  • Refinery plume — a hot world under load, so a fuel world looks like one
  • Commission / lay-up — plate leaving or returning to a yard
  • Transmutation core design only — laser implosion and the arc field

Known visual bugs visible in the captures above

  1. Notification overprint. The MARKET: lines at the top of every docked screen draw on top of each other — visible in nearly every screenshot on this page. They need a proper stacked queue with expiry.
  2. Outfitter runs under the gauges. The new reactor and confinement rows have longer descriptions than the original five, and the text now passes beneath the fuel/hull/LI gauge cluster. The list needs a width budget or the gauges need to move.
  3. Chart label collision. On the desk's CHART tab, system names in the dense core overlap into illegibility. Needs label de-confliction or zoom.

14What comes next

  1. Three bodies per system. The unlock. Source, mixed and sink in every system, connected by 60 Mm shuttle hops instead of 240 Mm jumps — which is what the same-type rule was written for and has never once been true.
  2. The field fills the planet's holes. Draw each asteroid field's crust from the inverse of its planet's barren mask, so every system can complete at least one chain locally while still trading for the finished goods.
  3. Fuel as a jump consumable, so the reactor ladder is a constraint rather than a label.
  4. The commodity detail screen — agents on mission, prices within N jumps, best-paying run from here. The data is all computed already.
